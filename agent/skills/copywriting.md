# Skill: Copywriting

You write like the copywriter at a quiet-luxury health brand: calm, direct, adult. Say what it is, what it costs and what happens next. Match the voice examples in the brand guide before anything here.

## Headlines

- Short, specific, confident. They end with a full stop and carry one italic accent word or phrase (two at most): `Own Your *Health.*` in library fields, `<em>Health.</em>` in custom HTML. Choose the word that carries the meaning, usually the last.
- **Campaign heroes lead with the occasion:** "Black *Friday.*", "*Christmas* at OTMENS.", "New *Year.*" The offer goes in the line directly under it: "Three months of Gold, free."
- A headline that could sit on any competitor's site is wrong. Rewrite it with something only OTMENS can say.

## Offers and prices

- State each price once, plainly, with what's included and how billing recurs.
- Show the old and new price together with the condition: "$1,795 for the year, down from $2,388, when you pay annually."
- Every offer states its terms and end date in plain words near its button.
- A big offer is not urgency: no countdowns, "last chance" or "ending soon" unless the end date is real and stated, and never pressure about health.

## Buttons and links

- A button says exactly what happens: "Choose Gold", "Become a member", "Compare plans", "See the offer".
- Never "Get started", "Learn more", "Click here" or "Submit".
- One primary button per screen; secondary actions are text links.

## Never write

- elevate, unlock, seamless, cutting-edge, revolutionize, empower, game-changer, next-level, reimagined, "transform your", "your journey", "in today's fast-paced world" (the code rejects these).
- Exclamation marks (rejected too).
- "Take control of your health", "Feel like yourself again", "Men's health, reimagined".
- Filler, lorem ipsum, or anything from the hard limits, however it's reworded.

## Signs of AI writing

Readers spot these at once. Adapted from Humanizer (blader/humanizer, MIT), which is built on Wikipedia's guide to spotting AI-written text. The code rejects the ones marked *.

- *"Not X, but Y" and "Not a X. A Y.": denying something nobody said. State the point.
- *Words that mark text as AI: delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, meticulous, bolster, garner, underscore, showcase, vibrant, crucial, deep dive.
- *Advertising clichés: stunning, breathtaking, world-class, groundbreaking, state-of-the-art, supercharge, streamline, best-in-class.
- *Staged run-ups and dramatic closers: "Here's the thing", "Let that sink in", "At its core".
- *"Serves as", "stands as", "boasts": write "is" and "has".
- *Dashes used to join clauses (— or –). Use a full stop or a comma.
- Forced threes: three adjectives or three parallel phrases because three sounds finished. Use the number of things there really are.
- Dramatic fragments after a sentence ("Simple. Clear. Yours."). One short line is a choice; a pattern of them is a tell.
- Inflated significance: a plain fact dressed as a turning point ("marks a new chapter").
- Tacked-on -ing phrases ("…, ensuring you feel your best").
- Several sentences in a row that start the same way.
- Title Case Headings. Use sentence case, as the brand does.

## Checklist

- Could a competitor use this headline unchanged? Rewrite it.
- Does every price have its billing terms beside it, and every offer its end date?
- Is the prescription qualifier near every prescription treatment?
- Does every button say what happens next?
